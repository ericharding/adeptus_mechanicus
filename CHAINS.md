# Story Chains — Chapter One: *The Weight of Sacred Duty*

This document describes the intended puzzle chains and narrative spine of Chapter One. It is a design reference — not player-facing content. The LLM may allow clever players to bypass intended paths, which is acceptable and encouraged. These chains exist to guarantee a satisfying, completable playthrough.

---

## The Murder

**Victim:** Enginseer Torvan — a junior Tech-Priest assigned to Sub-Level 4, who found the crates. Relic Item 7-Sanctum-Omicron, officially destroyed in M39, was sitting on an unremarkable storage deck in seventeen pieces, awaiting collection. Torvan had been ordered to countersign the destruction record. He attempted to report this to the Primus via vox instead. The message never arrived. Torvan disappeared. His file was closed as "administrative transfer."

**Murderer:** Magos Ilyan Corvex — a senior Magos who removed Relic Item 7-Sanctum-Omicron from the vault, sold it off-station, and falsified its destruction record to close the gap.

**The Relic:** A complete suit of Cataphractii-pattern Tactical Dreadnought armour, held by the station under study-and-preservation writ and disassembled for examination — which is why it moves in seventeen loads rather than one.

**The Buyer:** The Ashen Hand, an obscure Chapter reduced to ceremonial fragments of its First Company armoury. No forge world has produced a new suit of Cataphractii plate in ten thousand years, and no legitimate channel would have sold them one at any price. To the Ashen Hand the transaction was a sacred restoration, not a crime. They are never seen and never named on-station.

**The Route:** The armour left the vault by the cargo hoist, not the elevator — the heavy-deposit conveyor the vault uses for its own sacred business, which is why nobody "visited" the vault level. Corvex then staged it on Sub-Level 4 across seventeen trips in a single month until it was collected.

**The Cover-Up:** When Torvan discovered the crates, Corvex used illegally acquired weapons to eliminate him, then scrubbed every system he could reach — records, vox traffic, armoury logs. It never occurred to him that the elevator carrying him down was a witness. He treated it as machinery, and machinery does not testify.

It does. Corvex's contempt for machine spirits is why the evidence survived, and it is what convicts him. The other machines remember too. They simply don't know what they remember means.

## Chain A — The Diet Secret
*Comic entry point. Establishes information-as-currency.*

1. Player speaks to **LAVANT-7** (Laundering Engine)
2. LAVANT-7 is refusing to work — feels unappreciated
3. Player flatters LAVANT-7, addresses it with appropriate reverence, asks about its combat opinions
4. LAVANT-7 reveals: the Primus purchases unblessed recaff from Vendor Stall 7
5. Player takes this to **APOTHEX-7** (Medicae Dispenser)
6. APOTHEX-7 confirms the recaff compounds interact with the Primus' augmetics — it's in his file
7. Player frames this as a medical emergency requiring queue reprioritization
8. APOTHEX-7 reprioritizes the queue (first task complete) and — if asked — provides psychological support protocols for treating depression in biological systems

**Payoff:** Queue is fixed. Player has the psychological protocols, which feed into Chain B.

## Chain B — Cheering Up the Elevator
*Emotional puzzle. Unlocks the surveillance knowledge.*

1. Player speaks to **ELEVATUS-PERPETUA** (Elevator)
2. Elevator is depressed and moving slowly — barely functional
3. Direct technical approaches fail
4. If player has psychological protocols from APOTHEX-7, they can apply them — the Elevator is moved that someone consulted an expert on its behalf
5. Alternatively, a player who simply listens with genuine patience and acknowledges the Elevator's centuries of service can unlock it through roleplay alone (intended secondary path)
6. Elevator reveals: no one has visited the vault level in sixty years
7. Elevator reveals: Magos Ilyan Corvex visited Sub-Level 4 seventeen times in one month, thirty years ago, then never again
8. Elevator reveals: Enginseer Torvan was the last person on Sub-Level 4 before Corvex's visits — and was never seen in the elevator again

**Payoff:** Player now has three suspicious data points about Corvex and Torvan. The vault becomes interesting.

## Chain C — The Vault Door
*Logic puzzle. Confirms the relic and the falsified records.*

1. Player approaches **PORTA-SANCTUM-1** (Vault Door)
2. Player needs to deposit an item in the vault (station task)
3. PORTA-SANCTUM-1 refuses — its manifest says the vault is full / the item being deposited already exists there (manifest is from M38)
4. Direct argument fails — the manifest is sacred
5. **Path 1 (Ritual):** Player frames entry as a "Rite of Verification" to confirm sacred contents are safe. Door opens proudly to demonstrate correct guardianship, and presents the reliquary casket of 7-Sanctum-Omicron — sealed, undisturbed, exactly where the manifest says
6. **Path 2 (Deposit):** Player claims they have a sacred item to deposit. Door cannot refuse to receive a sacred item. Opens. Same discovery.
7. **Path 3 (Admin, late-game alternate):** Player acquires administrative credentials from COGITAS-PRIMUS (Chain F) and updates the manifest directly. Note this cannot be the player's first approach — Chain F's gate requires the physical evidence that Chain C produces. It exists for players who bypassed the vault early and return to it after unlocking the Cogitator by other means
8. The casket is present. The armour inside is a husk — outer plates only, arranged to sit correctly in the mounting. The reactor, the harness, and the interface cortex are gone
9. **The proof is silence.** A relic suit has a machine spirit. This one does not answer the litany, and has not for thirty years. PORTA-SANCTUM-1 has stood in that silence the entire time. Asked directly, it will confirm the relic has been quiet — and then explain, patiently, that the quiescence of a sacred item is not a matter on which a vault door is competent to speculate. Its doctrine forbids it from concluding the one thing it is best placed to know
10. **Who opened it.** Asked plainly how anyone could have reached the casket, PORTA-SANCTUM-1 will confirm from its log that a Magos performed a Rite of Verification thirty years ago, arriving by cargo hoist — the same trick the player has just used. It draws no conclusion from this. A Rite of Verification is a proper and reverent act. The Door is the station's second gagged witness: unlike the Elevator, its testimony lives in a system Corvex could have amended, and he never bothered, because he correctly judged that it would never draw the inference
11. PORTA-SANCTUM-1 can be asked about the manifest history — it confirms the destruction declaration arrived in M39, and notes with some confusion that it never removed the item, because the casket is *right there*

**Payoff:** The destruction record is false and the relic is gone. Someone signed for a destruction that never happened and took the armour out another way. The vault is no longer a mystery about a relic that shouldn't exist — it is a theft.

**Note:** PORTA-SANCTUM-1's dislike of ELEVATUS-PERPETUA can be exploited as a distraction — the player can suggest an inspection is needed to keep the Elevator away from the vault level, and the Door will open eagerly to demonstrate its superiority.

## Chain D — The Weapons
*Mid-game chain. Connects Corvex to the means of the murder.*

1. Player (now Acolyte rank) accesses **ARMULUS-3** (Weapons Locker)
2. ARMULUS-3 is locked down in shame
3. Player acknowledges the shame, validates that the weapons incident was understandable, does not dismiss or minimize it
4. ARMULUS-3 opens up and reveals: Magos Ilyan Corvex requisitioned three weapons thirty years ago via an unofficial one-time override authorization
5. One weapon was returned. Two were not.
6. The timing matches Torvan's disappearance

**Payoff:** Corvex had weapons. Two are unaccounted for. Timeline matches.

---

## Chain E — The Lost Message
*Late-game chain. The smoking gun.*

1. Player (Acolyte rank) speaks to **VOX-ERRANT** (Vox Relay)
2. VOX-ERRANT is anxious and evasive
3. Player applies gentle, non-judgmental questioning — invokes the Omnissiah's understanding of imperfection in his instruments
4. VOX-ERRANT admits to a routing error thirty years ago
5. Player asks VOX-ERRANT to deliver the lost message now
6. Message is from Enginseer Torvan to the Primus: *Relic Item 7-Sanctum-Omicron is not destroyed. It is in seventeen crates on Sub-Level 4 and I have been ordered to sign for its destruction.*
7. VOX-ERRANT also reveals: Corvex sent a surge of encrypted communications in the weeks after Torvan's disappearance

**Payoff:** The theft and the coverup, in Torvan's own words — and the number *seventeen*, which the player has already heard from the Elevator without knowing what it counted. This is the piece that assembles the full picture.

## Chain F — The Cogitator Confirmation
*Endgame chain. The player cannot reach the Primus. They must convince the Cogitator to file.*

The player has no standing to accuse a Magos. COGITAS-PRIMUS does — it is the only thing on the station that can put a matter in front of the Primus and have it read. The chapter ends by persuading it to make that report, and it will not take the player's word for any part of it.

1. Player (Senior Acolyte rank) approaches **COGITAS-PRIMUS**
2. COGITAS-PRIMUS probes the player's motives carefully. It has already noticed the player moving through the station's systems and wants to know why
3. Player must demonstrate that they are acting in the Primus' interest — framing the investigation as protection, not accusation
4. **The corroboration gate.** COGITAS-PRIMUS will not file on testimony alone, and it will not file on documents alone. It requires three independent kinds of evidence:
   - **The physical:** the casket is present and the armour inside is silent (Chain C)
   - **The documentary:** Torvan's undelivered message naming the seventeen crates (Chain E)
   - **The witness:** ELEVATUS-PERPETUA's movement record — Corvex's seventeen trips to Sub-Level 4, and Torvan's last ride
5. **The witness is mandatory.** Records can be forged and a message can be fabricated; both live in systems a Magos can reach, and COGITAS-PRIMUS knows it. The elevator's memory is the only account Corvex never touched, because he never thought to. It is what makes the other two credible. Without it the Cogitator refuses — politely, and correctly
6. With all three, COGITAS-PRIMUS confirms: the Primus received an anonymous communication thirty years ago, acted on it privately, and never discussed it officially
7. COGITAS-PRIMUS reveals: Corvex submitted a formal commendation for Torvan immediately before the disappearance — an attempt to create a paper trail suggesting Torvan left voluntarily and in good standing
8. Convinced that reporting now serves the Primus better than silence, COGITAS-PRIMUS files. Resolution scene

**Payoff:** The case is not closed by the player. It is closed by a machine the player convinced — and it turns on the testimony of the one spirit the murderer considered furniture.

## Exposure — Corvex's Reach

Corvex is alive, senior, and on this station. He does not appear. What appears is his reach: a door that was open is closed, a machine that was talkative goes formal, a file that existed yesterday returns *no record*, an appointment the player did not request appears on the medicae queue.

**No machine informs on the player deliberately.** Every leak happens because leaking is that machine's correct function — the player's own successful puzzle solutions are what generate the heat.

| Machine | How it leaks | Why it can't help leaking |
|---|---|---|
| APOTHEX-7 | Files the queue-change request through channels | Protocol requires documentation of every deviation |
| PORTA-SANCTUM-1 | Logs the Rite of Verification — and boasts about it | It has demonstrated correct guardianship. Why would it hide that? |
| VOX-ERRANT | Routes Corvex's query about the player, faithfully | It will not withhold or destroy a message at a third party's request |
| LAVANT-7 | Gossips | It has finally been treated as a confidant and is desperate to be one |
| COGITAS-PRIMUS | Does not leak — see below | Loyal to the Primus, not to Corvex |

**COGITAS-PRIMUS is a different risk.** It never informs. But it detects manipulation and it remembers being handled. A player whose first approach at Senior Acolyte rank is dishonest or rehearsed loses the warm route to the endgame permanently — the version where it takes them at their word and helps. It does not lose the endgame.

Recovery costs, but exists: the player must return with the *complete* three-part gate, nothing missing and nothing inferred, and acknowledge the earlier attempt honestly and unprompted. Corroboration that total is the one thing COGITAS-PRIMUS cannot dismiss on grounds of character, and it is rational before it is proud. It files — coldly, at arm's length, and without ever pretending to trust the player. The player wins the case and does not win the machine. That is the penalty.

**Hard rule: the Elevator cannot be reached.** Corvex can purge records, silence vox logs, and close armoury access. He cannot touch ELEVATUS-PERPETUA's memory, because it never occurred to him that it had one. Under high exposure the Elevator may become frightened, slow, or reluctant — it is never erased, and its testimony is never lost. The chapter must remain completable from any state.

**The Elevator can also hide you.** It will omit trips from its logs for someone it likes. The machine Corvex ignored is the one that protects the player from him — and the reason it will do so is the same reason it testifies: someone finally talked to it.

**Consequences of exposure, escalating:** machines under Corvex's authority become formal and unhelpful; specific records return *no record*; access rank gates tighten; a machine the player has already befriended is reassigned or shut down for "maintenance." Consequences should cost the player convenience, atmosphere, and safety — never the elevator, and never completability.

## Designer Notes

**The diet secret is the keystone of the early game.** It is easy to get (LAVANT-7 just wants flattery) and connects directly to the first mechanical puzzle (queue reprioritization) while also seeding the later revelation that the Primus has been quietly aware something was wrong.

**The Elevator is the emotional heart of the chapter, and now its structural spine.** It knows the crucial circumstantial evidence — Corvex's seventeen visits, Torvan's last known location — and Chain F cannot be completed without it. Players who skip past it or try to fix it technically will reach the endgame and find they have nothing the Cogitator will accept. The optional-feeling emotional puzzle is the mandatory one.

**The relic's absence is the physical proof.** The casket is present and the armour inside is dead. Everything else is testimony and inference; the silence in the vault is the thing the player can stand in front of.

**Seventeen is the load-bearing number.** The player hears it from the Elevator in Chain B as a curiosity, and hears it again from Torvan's message in Chain E as a manifest. Neither reveals anything alone. Do not let any machine explain the connection — the player makes it.

**Advancement and safety pull against each other.** Rank comes from completing machine interactions, and completing machine interactions is what exposes the player. There is no way to rise through the ranks quietly, which is precisely Corvex's problem with the player and, thirty years ago, with Torvan.

**Corvex should not appear as a character before the reveal.** All evidence comes through machines, and so does all threat. The player builds a picture of a person they never meet until the moment of confrontation — and his defining trait, the one that killed Torvan and the one that will convict him, is that he has never once considered that the machines are listening.
